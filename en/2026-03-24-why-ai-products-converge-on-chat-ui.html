AI chat UI: when to add an editor or a visible plan

Describing a goal, selecting an edit, and checking the current draft or plan are different tasks. Compare Canvas, Pages, Artifacts, and Cocoa to decide where conversation benefits from direct manipulation.

AI Strategy Research cutoff March 24, 2026 Updated October 3, 2026
LLMAI AgentChat InterfaceProduct DesignWorkflowUX

The request is understood, but the edit target is not

Suppose an AI drafts a proposal and you want only its second paragraph shortened. You write “cut this to half the length,” but the assistant interprets “this” as another passage and changes material you wanted to preserve. You explain the location again, compare versions, and restore the lost wording. This hypothetical example separates describing a change from identifying its target.

Conversation lets a consultation start before the goal is precise. Once a draft exists and the edit target is known, however, the flexibility of describing a goal gives way to the need to point accurately. An input method that helps at the beginning need not suit every later action.

This article compares product announcements and research available by March 24, 2026, examining why conversation can remain the entry point while drafts, shared versions, and editable plans become visible. The criterion is what users can see and correct after making a request. A one-off consultation and repeated revisions of drafts or plans also carry different costs of learning additional controls.

Conversation accepts requests before their goal is settled

Before writing a proposal, even the appropriate output may be undecided. You ask what to explain to a customer, inspect suggestions, then request a comparison table with cost conditions. A fixed input form would need separate operations for such changes of purpose. Conversation can keep consultation, creation, and correction within an exchange of language.

OpenAI's Apps announcement describes invoking apps through natural language and using interactive maps or playlists inside a conversation. Microsoft's Copilot Chat announcement also presents agents accessible from chat. These are product examples of one entry point leading into different kinds of work.

I interpret that breadth as a design advantage when a goal changes during a task. It does not establish that writing a request is faster when users already know the available choices. Nor do a few announcements measure adoption across the market. Compare which stage's burden conversation can reduce rather than assigning its popularity to one cost or usability advantage.

Use language for the change and selection for its target

For the opening example, selecting the second paragraph and asking for a shorter version avoids restating its location. OpenAI's canvas announcement describes selecting passages, directly editing text or code, and restoring earlier versions. Gemini Canvas's announcement also shows highlighting a paragraph to adjust concision or tone.

Conversation and selection supply different information. “Use simpler language for this customer” expresses the intended change; “this paragraph” identifies the object. Selecting it could reduce repeated paragraph numbers or quoted passages. Test that possibility by comparing target-selection tasks on the same draft.

Selection does not establish that the edit is substantively correct. Removing a sentence containing a condition can alter meaning even within the selected passage. Users need to inspect the change and retained conditions, then restore the previous text if necessary. Precise targeting and judging the revised content remain different tasks.

A visible artifact and a team's current version serve different needs

Repeated revisions create another task: deciding which response contains the accepted text. Anthropic's announcement introducing Artifacts describes code, documents, and web designs appearing in a dedicated window alongside conversation. It provides a product example of working with a visible artifact beyond reading a response.

If another person edits the draft, visibility alone is insufficient. Microsoft's Copilot Pages announcement describes retaining AI-generated content in a page that people can edit, extend, and share. An artifact for personal inspection and an object for collaborative updates should be compared separately.

In the hypothetical proposal task, retaining the accepted text in one place while colleagues update it could reduce explanations about which version to use. Exporting and emailing a file is different: revising the original surface need not update the recipient's copy. Decide whether the shared page, exported file, or sent attachment is authoritative.

This distinction also prevents similar product labels from obscuring different capabilities. Dedicated display, personal editing, and joint updates are separate things to check. A feature announcement cannot quantify the reduction in version mistakes. Test the handoff through the point where the recipient opens the intended content.

Sometimes the research plan needs revision before the prose does

A draft's problem may lie in the research sequence rather than its wording. Cocoa v1 proposes collaboratively editing and executing plans in a document. Intermediate work becomes an editable object alongside the final text.

Its study had 16 researchers spend 25 minutes per condition on literature-informed tasks. Both interfaces used GPT-4o and the same tools; chat also had a document editor. Median five-point self-reported steerability was 3 for chat and 4 for Cocoa, with a significant difference. Ease of use and general output utility showed no significant difference. These results do not measure business completion rates or speed.

A visible plan can support replacing the competitor-research step while retaining the customer requirements already collected. But changing the earlier research while retaining conclusions derived from it does not restore consistency. The interface needs to expose which step changed and which dependent passages require review. This is a design example informed by the research.

A short consultation may not benefit from learning a planning interface. Separate work requiring intervention in individual steps from work seeking an immediate answer. A non-significant difference also does not prove equivalence; retain the limits of the tasks and measurements.

Analysis can require several conditions to be visible together

Consider checking a proposal's cost table while changing customer segment, reporting period, and calculation method. A conversation-only design can place an earlier condition and a new aggregation in separate responses. Reading the wrong table for the selected conditions invalidates the comparison even if its explanation sounds fluent.

The Keyhole Effect offers a theoretical account of how hidden conditions and displaced content burden understanding in multi-step, state-dependent analysis. It presents design patterns and testable hypotheses, rather than a new controlled experiment measuring chat's inferiority. I use it to motivate evaluating how well users can inspect conditions and results together.

Placing the period and filters beside a table can make its assumptions easier to check. Yet if the displayed conditions differ from those used in the calculation, a polished screen could make errors easier to miss. Check both the visibility of the assumptions and their actual application.

Showing everything on one screen is not the answer either. Unused settings and old tables create more objects to search through. Compare a conversation-only design with one that keeps the current comparison's conditions and results together while making earlier versions available when needed.

Repeated requests can also benefit from a form

The first proposal may require discussion of its purpose, while a weekly report may already have a fixed period, owner, and destination. A screen for checking those fields could fit better than rewriting them in prose. Using conversation as an entry point and making every input free text are separate choices.

Cowork's February 24, 2026 announcement describes commands for reports or dashboards launching with structured forms. It shows a conversational product adding field-based input for repeated requests. This does not establish a general usability advantage for forms; choose according to how consistently the input can be structured.

Displaying a required reporting period creates an opportunity to notice an omission. But retaining the previous period as a default can introduce another mistake if the user proceeds without checking. Compare required fields, defaults, and review after entry with a free-text request. A design can combine conversation for exceptions with fields for recurring conditions.

Show executing actions separately from completed text

When a request expands from preparing a report to delivering it, the draft is no longer the only object to inspect. The ChatGPT agent announcement describes on-screen narration, interruptions, and browser takeover. It offers a product example of users intervening in work started through conversation.

A plan saying “save,” an ongoing save operation, and a saved result should not collapse into one status. In the proposal example, the editable version, an unsent recipient, and the saved file are different inspection targets. When the request changes, distinguish actions still open to revision from actions already performed.

Change the goal in conversation

Express an intention such as “make this internal-facing.” Check which draft and planned actions should receive the change.

Revise part of a draft or plan

Select the passage or step. Inspect its revision and the later material that depends on its result.

Check the work's result

Verify the accepted version, saved destination, and executed actions. A plan's wording alone is not completion.

These roles are this article's proposal, not a requirement for three separate screens. They can be implemented by placing an artifact beside conversation or adding conversation to an existing editor. Place information near the particular unit the user needs to correct.

Evaluate usability through revisions after the request

For the opening proposal, test discussing its goal, shortening one paragraph, and handing the accepted version to a colleague. Record repeated explanations needed to identify the target, lost conditions, and whether unchanged passages require rereading. Verify that the recipient opens the same version to separate personal editing from handoff costs. This is a proposed evaluation, not a measured product ranking.

An unsettled consultation, a local edit in a long document, analysis across several conditions, and a routine report have different burdens. With the same task and model, compare conversation alone, an editor with selectable targets, and an editable plan. Look beyond the speed of the first draft to how people find errors, repair them, and pass work on.

Conversation can remain a place to develop a request that is not yet precise. Once the edit target is known, test whether pointing directly to the passage or step helps. I recommend combining controls around what users need to see and correct during work, rather than declaring chat the final product form. The person who delegated the proposal should be able to fix the intended paragraph without explaining the entire document again.
