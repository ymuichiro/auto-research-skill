Move a discovered agent into its first real task

Suppose a salesperson finds an agent that organizes meeting notes. Its demo neatly presents summaries and next steps. Their own notes, however, say that adoption will be considered next time and that pricing remains unconfirmed. Recording these as commitments or agreed prices in the CRM would turn a useful-looking summary into misinformation for the next owner.

This hypothetical scenario examines adoption decisions. The person wanting a trial, the administrator reviewing customer-data connections, and the buyer checking prices are asking different questions about the same product. Listing, connection, permission, and task value should not collapse into a single decision.

Using material available by April 27, 2026, this article compares discovery, distribution, and procurement routes across five vendors. I would assess model capability together with whether a listing can be translated into concrete trial conditions. Explaining the selection rationale, allowed scope, and completion criteria lets a user propose the first task.

OpenAI: discover an app during work and call it in conversation

OpenAI's October 6, 2025 Apps announcement describes contextual suggestions, calling apps by name, and interactive interfaces within conversations. An example moves from an outline to creating a presentation. Users can encounter an option through the work they are doing, alongside searching for a service elsewhere.

The December 17 submission and directory announcement adds searchable listings and developer review submissions containing MCP connectivity, testing instructions, metadata, and country availability. Connection includes disclosure of shared data types and the privacy policy. Discovery in conversation is connected to a route through which providers explain their apps for publication.

Passing publication review and authorizing the use of an organization's customer notes are separate decisions. In our scenario, check shared information and output destinations, then test treatment of ambiguous statements. Convenient discovery during a conversation becomes more useful when the delegated task is equally specific.

Anthropic: connect familiar services to Claude

Anthropic's July 14, 2025 connector directory announcement provides a discovery route for remote services and local desktop extensions. It distinguishes authenticating a connection from installing a desktop extension. At launch, remote connections also had a paid-plan requirement.

The announcement illustrates creating release notes from actual Linear tickets. The agent moves from generating a generic template to reading records from the user's everyday work. This is a vendor-provided usage example, not an independently measured result for writing speed or accuracy.

For meeting notes, wanting access to an existing service and wanting to procure a complete ready-made agent lead to different searches. A connector is best understood here as an entry point for handling that service's information or operations through Claude. A listed tool does not necessarily provide a finished meeting-note workflow.

Microsoft: connect the maker, administrator, and user

Microsoft's May 22, 2025 Agent Store announcement presents Microsoft, partner, and organization-built agents with descriptions, sharing, and publishing routes. An internal agent can reach a place where users discover it, instead of remaining only with its maker.

The administration documentation pinned before the cutoff describes reviewing data, actions, publishers, and certification information, then selecting users or groups. Internally built agents pass through maker submission and administrator review before publication. This is a historical document snapshot; it does not mean the store first launched in April.

For an internal sales agent, a person familiar with the work could define instructions, an administrator could review connections and distribution, and salespeople could use it. Certification information in an administration screen does not settle how well ambiguous notes will be interpreted. Distribution decisions and task quality need to be assessed together.

Google Cloud and AWS: compare what happens after purchase

Google Cloud's October 14, 2025 Marketplace and Gemini Enterprise article describes employees discovering candidates, requesting IT procurement, and administrators registering purchased agents. Providers can supply an A2A Agent Card from which capabilities and endpoints are ingested. Catalog information becomes evidence for registration in the intended work environment.

AWS's July 16 AI agents and tools announcement similarly describes natural-language discovery, MCP or A2A and deployment metadata, purchase, and deployment. Routes include AgentCore Runtime deployment and adding tools to Gateway, according to the offering. Procurement and deployment differ from simply connecting a user's existing account.

The October 31 AWS update adds contract and usage pricing for Runtime containers and simpler OAuth setup for API products. Delivery form changes the environment to operate and the costs to examine. Matching use-case labels do not make the work after purchase identical.

Integration validation described by both vendors can help narrow candidates. Distinguishing a commitment from a proposal, or filling the required CRM fields, still needs task-specific checks. Agent Cards and protocol labels describe connections; they do not set the passing criteria for an organization's sales workflow.

Ask the AI comparing products to explain its recommendation

AWS's November 30, 2025 agent mode announcement describes expressing requirements in conversation, comparing candidates, and generating a purchase proposal for internal approval. AI assists the work of selecting agents. Preparing a comparison and authorizing a purchase remain separate decisions.

For our task, specify more than “sales assistance.” Require uncommitted items to remain tentative and exclude CRM writes from the trial. If the listing does not explain support for a requirement, leave it unverified. A fluent comparison table should not invent capabilities absent from the provider's description.

I would ask what evidence makes a candidate fit, what remains missing, and what trial would settle the choice. Estimates should include connection preparation and human corrections alongside price. Assess whether AI's proposal makes the decision easier for the person responsible.

Four checks to turn a listing into one trial

Define completion

Organize agreements, unresolved questions, next owners, and deadlines. Fluent summarization alone is insufficient.

Specify inputs and outputs

Identify notes, matching customers, and the draft destination. Specify CRM updates or external sending separately if included.

Define users and access

Choose trial users and data scope. Reconcile the provider, destinations, and requested permissions with the product description.

Compare cost and deployment

Check contracts, model use, deployment, preparation, and correction effort. Different pricing units should not become a simple price ranking.

This is the article's trial design, not a common vendor specification. Existing approved services used within their current scope may not require new procurement. Make the relevant decisions specific, so owners can avoid repeatedly performing reviews unrelated to the intended use.

Check candidate quality with the same meeting notes

I recommend starting with a small group and test data, using the same notes as the existing manual process. Include commitments, proposals, undated follow-ups, and similarly named customers. Define completion before beginning. This is a proposed trial, not a product comparison already conducted.

Inspect whether outputs invent commitments, hide uncertainty, or confuse owners and customers. Also check access outside the permitted reading scope. If drafting is sufficient for the task, value can be tested within that boundary without adding update operations.

Include connection preparation, review waiting time, user corrections, and usable results in the adoption assessment. A model comparison should match conditions beyond the model; a product comparison should record the available routes and costs of each product. Faster approval does not reduce workload if corrections remain substantial.

If quality is sufficient but the trial cannot begin, identify the unresolved decision. Can users find candidates? Is connection scope unclear? Are pricing units incompatible? This separates the need for further model evaluation from the need to explain adoption conditions better.

Include changes and withdrawal in the trial

I recommend recording purpose, users, destinations, actions, and the version reviewed. If a product approved for drafts later requests CRM write access, reconsider whether the same decision still applies. This is an operational proposal, not a claim that all stores automate identical reapproval.

Distinguish blocking store availability, disconnecting existing access, and stopping active work. Revoke provider or destination credentials when needed. Disappearing from a listing does not establish cancellation of running operations or reversal of data already shared.

Tell users why access stopped and where unfinished drafts should go. A design that supports withdrawal should also explain the state of delegated work. Compare convenient discovery together with the procedure for deciding what to do after changes.

Choose an entry point for the actual adoption bottleneck

To use existing service information in conversation, start with connection choices and data-sharing explanations. To distribute an internal workflow, follow maker submission, administrator review, and user distribution. To buy a partner product, compare price, delivery form, and registration in the intended environment.

A ready-made product meeting the trial conditions may avoid a new internal build. If customer identification or handling uncertain commitments is missing, compare configuration or additional implementation with building independently. Neither store availability nor internal ownership alone supplies a reason to adopt.

The useful goal is a person who can explain more than wanting the product: they can identify the task, scope, and result that would justify wider use. Connect capability evaluation to departmental adoption. A catalog and distribution route that help articulate this proposal can move a discovered candidate into its first real task.
