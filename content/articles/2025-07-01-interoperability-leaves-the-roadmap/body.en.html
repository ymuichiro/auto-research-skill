Question

What does interoperability actually make exchangeable?

A2A, MCP, and OpenAPI releases in spring and summer 2025 made particular forms of connection testable. A2A addresses tasks, state, and artifacts exchanged between agents; MCP addresses connections to tools and data; OpenAPI describes operations. Their availability does not mean a connected participant is interchangeable in business terms. This article separates connection compatibility from interchangeability of output meaning, authentication scope, failure behavior, and approval responsibility.

What connection exchanges

Tools and resources, tasks and artifacts and status, or operation inputs and outputs.

What connection does not exchange

Whose authority is used, what an output means, and who approves a consequential change must be defined separately.

What to test

Compare output schemas, authorization scope, timeouts, retries, and treatment of indeterminate state before and after a replacement.

Role of the published material

A2A, MCP, and managed platforms address distinct connection surfaces

The A2A announcement and its Linux Foundation donation treat agent cards, capability discovery, and long task lifecycles as open coordination concerns. Google Cloud MCP database integrations give a concrete tool and data connection case. Responses API remote MCP and Azure AI Foundry A2A/MCP/OpenAPI support offer options for combining managed execution and open connection formats.

These materials document formats and available scope. They do not prove compatible business outcomes, compliance with an organization’s security requirements, or equal performance between products. Evaluations such as tau-bench, which include tools, a user, and state, remind us that a shared interface still requires separate checks of final work state.

Hypothetical example

Compare output and failure contracts before replacing a search role

This is a design example. A search role called through A2A is replaced with another provider. Sending a task and receiving an artifact may work, yet one provider may return source URLs and document versions while the other returns prose only. One may report missing user permission while another returns an empty result. The conditions under which a user concludes “not found” have changed.

On identical inputs, compare output schema, evidence, authorization failure, timeout, retry, and audit record. If external delivery or data updates are involved, the user or organizational approval point must also remain explicit. Treat communication success as insufficient; test whether completion conditions remain intact.

Conclusion

Making interoperability a design premise means documenting the responsibilities that cannot be exchanged

Public specifications made connection to other agents and tools more testable. This does not mean interoperability is complete. Authentication, data meaning, responsibility after failure, and approval rules remain contracts of an application and organization. Comparing those contracts as well as the connection format is what makes a future replacement practical.
