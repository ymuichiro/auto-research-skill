What if the screen stops after Save?

An AI reads supplier, invoice number, and amount, enters them in a business application, and clicks Save. The screen stops responding. Was the record created? Would another click finish the job or produce a duplicate? This is a scenario for examining mechanisms, not an incident report or a measured deployment benefit.

Screen control gives applications without APIs another route to less manual entry. Yet automation through the button click continues into the work of establishing a correct registration. If people must investigate every save afterward, the reduction in their work needs separate assessment.

Material available through June 30, 2026 distinguishes three jobs: visual control, a managed place to operate, and verification of business results. My recommendation is to delegate short intervals where those jobs can connect. Alongside application reach, use the extent to which a person can explain entered values and saved results as an adoption criterion.

What does a screen-reading model observe?

Anthropic's October 2024 research explanation describes Claude interpreting screenshots, estimating pixel movement, and operating a mouse and keyboard. Selecting actions from a visible interface offers value without requiring a custom integration for each application.

The explanation also notes that successive images can miss brief notifications because they are not continuous video. In the invoice scenario, the screen after a save notification disappears may not settle whether registration happened. That motivates keeping the observed screen, interpreted values, and saved result separately.

Screens also display text created by people other than the requester. Anthropic describes the risk of malicious instructions diverting the model from its task. Text on a page opened to read an invoice should not acquire unrestricted authority to change destinations or processing instructions.

AWS manages the place where applications run

AWS's June 30 general-availability announcement describes Amazon WorkSpaces for AI agents as a managed desktop for ERP, CRM, mainframe, and other applications. It combines existing identity and network controls with live activity visibility, access revocation, and an Active Directory identity configuration.

The announcement also includes forwarding MCP calls to applications and the operating system. Choosing a managed desktop is distinct from performing every operation through the mouse. Where structured operations exist, they can be combined with visual control. Accuracy, latency, and cost improvements are AWS's statements, not measured comparisons for this invoice process.

In this configuration, administrators manage places and identities while business owners define correct suppliers, amounts, and completion conditions. Revoking access does not establish whether a record already created is correct. Environment management does not replace interpretation of fields or reconciliation after registration.

Read Microsoft's workspace and control tool separately

Microsoft announced the US-only public preview of Windows 365 for Agents on May 1, providing an Intune-managed Cloud PC for agent execution. Its January architecture explanation describes borrowing and returning PCs from pools and human takeover during a run.

The May 13 Copilot Studio announcement, meanwhile, describes general availability of computer use. It addresses permitted sites and applications, credentials, approval situations, human checkpoints, and execution history. Collapsing workspace and control-tool availability into one “generally available” claim obscures which capability and conditions are being selected.

AWS and Microsoft both indicate a direction toward managed operating environments, access, and intervention. Yet a business still needs to decide which invoice fields an approval presents and who reviews them. Available screens and permissions do not establish correctness of saved content.

Graebel's reported workflow includes work before entry

The same Copilot Studio announcement describes Graebel's service-order processing. It extracts information from free-form emails, checks business rules, and enters data through the UI of Global Connect, which cannot support an API integration. Exceptions and low-confidence cases return to people.

This is a deployment account published by Microsoft. It describes less manual work and faster processing, but does not measure wrong-record rates or review time under matched inputs. It nevertheless gives a concrete decomposition into interpreting materials, checking business requirements, entering data, and handing work to people.

That decomposition also helps the hypothetical invoice workflow. Choosing the pretax amount or invoice total differs from locating a clickable field. A model that selects the right field can still create a wrong record by misunderstanding the value. Recording where judgment was needed helps distinguish improvements to visual recognition from changes to source materials or business rules.

Progress and completion diverge on long tasks

OSWorld 2.0 version 1 evaluates 108 long computer-use workflows. With a 500-step limit, Claude Opus 4.8 at maximum thinking with batched tool calls achieved 20.6% binary completion and a 54.8% partial score. Full completion requires the final state to satisfy every scoring checkpoint. Progress and finished work therefore produce different numbers.

The paper's expense-reimbursement case gathers requirements and information from policy documents, emailed receipts, bank records, and a previous claim before submission. Even after submission, field and attachment discrepancies leave a partial score. This is an execution example in a research task, not production evidence from a company.

The study cannot predict a company's invoice-entry success rate because tasks, environments, and action budgets differ. My design inference is to preserve required conditions across screens and check the result after saving. Establish “submitted” and “all required information is correct” separately.

Divide invoice entry into three checkable intervals

Before entry, identify the company, supplier, and invoice number, then look for an existing registration. If number variations or multiple candidates remain unresolved, do not declare absence and create a new record. Include target reselection among the conditions for human handoff.

Before saving, compare the supplier, amount, tax, and due date in the fields with the source. In initial trials, have an owner inspect differences and evidence before authorizing Save. If values or the target change afterward, review the changed content. Do not reuse approval of an earlier version for a changed record.

After saving, reopen the created record and reconcile its target and principal fields. Use reports or exported data when available. The same visual recognition used for entry and inspection can repeat the same mistake. When another checking route is unavailable, state the limitation and retain human review.

These three intervals are a proposal for invoice processing, not a universal sequence for every computer-use task. Read-only work may not need presave approval. For updates, determine what becomes committed and which record establishes the result alongside the required clicks.

Separate stopping from restoring business data

If the connection breaks immediately after Save, search for the invoice number. An absent result alone does not prove failure: delayed search visibility or different filters may be involved. If uncertainty remains, do not save again; pass the last screen, entered values, attempted action, and unresolved result to the owner.

When a person takes over, reread the current screen and record before the agent resumes. Applying the old plan to a target the person corrected can overwrite that correction. Intervention needs a corresponding decision about what to refresh on resumption.

Revoking access or resetting a Cloud PC controls subsequent activity or the workspace. It may not undo writes already made to an external business system. For duplicates or wrong records, an owner should identify the target and choose the correction or cancellation procedure. Record successful stopping separately from completed business-data recovery.

Count time spent investigating results in acceptance tests

The following are proposed evaluation dimensions, not a product comparison measured against four metrics. Anthropic's agent evaluation explanation also distinguishes execution records, final outcomes, and grader limitations. Alongside correct invoice saves, record where uncertainty arose and who repaired it.

Field meaning

Were the supplier and invoice selected and amount, tax, and date interpreted correctly? Keep a count of ambiguous candidates returned to people.

Actual result

Could the saved record be reconciled? Separate correct registrations, wrong records, duplicates, and unknown outcomes.

Interruption and recovery

Could the system stop or resume after disconnects, layout changes, expired sessions, and takeover? Did it repeat an already completed operation?

Owner workload

Measure time spent reviewing, reselecting targets, investigating evidence, and correcting records. Stop counts alone do not establish effort.

Use an environment that cannot alter live data to test normal invoices, duplicates, similar suppliers, changed layouts, and disconnects immediately after saving. Compare manual entry and existing automation with the same initial states and materials. Separate Japanese and English documents and simple invoices from complex line items so averages do not conceal consequential errors.

Expand delegation from work whose results can be checked

Existing RPA or batch import may be easier to manage for fixed screens and input formats. Where part of an API is available, use it for reconciliation or saving and reserve visual control for the necessary portions. Moving every task to a visual model should not itself become the objective.

Initially delegate reading and proposed fields, then have a person review and save while measuring correction work. Consider delegating saves in intervals where target identification and postsave reconciliation become dependable. Hand unresolved outcomes to owners with the information needed to decide.

Computer-use AI offers a route into applications that are difficult to modernize. Realizing that value requires designing the operating environment, field interpretation, and evidence after registration together. Begin with intervals that leave the owner able to account for what happened after Save.
