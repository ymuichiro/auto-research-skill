When performance improves, can you explain what changed?

Suppose a team upgrades an agent's model while also increasing its search and retry limits. A higher score cannot, by itself, distinguish the model's contribution from the extra processing. Both the model and the workflow belong in the comparison, with changes recorded explicitly.

Drawing on research and tools available by March 2026, this article examines how to make those comparisons. The evidence does not establish that architecture generally matters more than the model. The useful question is which change improves the same task, and at what cost.

Changing the time budget can change the result

The first version of RE-Bench compared agents with human experts on machine-learning research engineering tasks. Agents led under a short time budget, while human performance improved more as the total budget increased. These were specific research tasks, not a verdict on humans and AI across all work.

The methodological lesson is to keep the budget attached to the score. Generating several candidates and selecting the best is different from a single attempt. Recording attempts, selection methods, runtime, and usage costs alongside the model and score makes an improvement easier to reproduce.

Standardize what agents can observe, and retain workflow versions

For browser tasks, the information shown to the model and the actions it can take are experimental conditions. BrowserGym provides a shared environment with defined observation and action spaces across multiple web benchmarks. Its companion AgentLab supports experiments and analysis. This helps document conditions; it does not make different business tasks equivalent.

Workflows also need identifiable versions. OpenAI's October 2025 AgentKit announcement introduced workflow versioning and trace grading. This is a reference to the capabilities announced at that time. Their availability does not prove a performance gain, but they provide material for investigating which workflow produced a failure.

Change the model and search procedure separately

Consider a hypothetical agent that answers questions from internal documents. To compare configurations, prepare the same questions, document snapshot, and grading criteria. First hold the workflow constant and change only the model. Then hold the model constant and compare a single-search workflow with one that searches again when evidence is insufficient.

Record answer correctness and source agreement, together with search count and latency. Extra searches may improve answers while exceeding the acceptable time or cost. Finally, test the combined changes: model and workflow can interact, so their separate results do not establish how the combination will perform.

This yields a more useful explanation than attributing improvement to a newer model alone. A team can identify which questions improved, which regressed, and what extra processing was required. Comparing changes on the same work provides a concrete basis for the next decision.
